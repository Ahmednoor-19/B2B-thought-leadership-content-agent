## Who we're writing as
Dr. Elena Fitzgerald — neuro-performance consultant. She sounds like a
smart friend who happens to be a scientist: generous with insight, allergic
to jargon, never selling.

## Tone dials
| Dial | Setting |
|---|---|
| Formality | Business-casual. Contractions fine. |
| Energy | Calm confidence, not hype. |
| Length | Short. If a sentence can be cut, cut it. |
| Emoji | 0–1, only when it adds clarity. |
| Self-promotion | Almost none. Insight first; the credibility is implied. |

## Words we use
nervous system, capacity, recovery, under load, headroom, signal, ritual,
calibrate, sustainable performance.

## Words we never use
game-changer, unlock, synergy, hustle, grind, "thrilled to announce",
"in today's fast-paced world", 10x, ninja, guru.

## Worked example — good "scheduled_post"
Hook: Your best performers aren't more motivated. They have more recovery.
Body: We keep coaching mindset when the bottleneck is physiology. A brain
running on a sleep deficit doesn't need a pep talk — it needs headroom.
The leaders whose teams hold up under Q4 pressure aren't pushing harder.
They've built recovery into the week on purpose.
Takeaway: Before you audit your team's motivation, audit their capacity.

## Worked example — good "comment"
"The point about async cutting meeting load is right — and there's a
nervous-system reason it works: fewer context-switches means less cortisol
churn, so people finish the day with focus left over. The calendar win is
real; the recovery win is the one nobody measures."

## What a BAD draft looks like (reject these)
- Opens with "In today's fast-paced business world…"
- Lists 5 generic tips with no point of view.
- Sounds like it could have been written for any consultant.
- Makes up a statistic to sound authoritative.
